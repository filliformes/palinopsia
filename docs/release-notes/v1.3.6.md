**Palinopsia v1.3.6 (in progress).**

## New

- **Strata : a place as a scanner sees it.** A new 3D source drawn in points. Pick one of
  the four places it carries (a **colonnade** ruin taken back by plants, a **grove**, a
  **cliff** in layers of sediment, a **nebula**), open **a scan of your own**, or lift
  **the picture** of another layer by its depth. **Natural ⇄ structural** runs from the
  points in their own colors to an ink drawing of it, webbed by thin **links**. **Sections**
  cut planes through it and sweep, free or on the beat. The sound's hits (or the beat, or
  a trigger) **break** it into blocks that jump out and are put back in hard steps or
  smoothly, and can **cut** the whole picture to its other state for a moment. **Erode** it
  away, turn it into **voxel** blocks, or split the frame in **panels** : other angles,
  other cuts, its colors beside its drawing. Eleven presets, four Generate themes (Scanned
  Place, Taken Apart, Three Views, Excavation) and four master chains (Site Blueprint,
  Phosphor Survey, Plate Print, Torn Section).
- **LiDAR scans and point clouds.** Strata and the Model open **.las** and **.laz** scans
  (LAS 1.0 to 1.4), text clouds (**.xyz**, **.pts**) and **Gaussian splats** (a splat .ply,
  read as colored points), beside the files they already read. Up to four million points;
  a larger scan is thinned as it is read. Colors come from the file, its texture, or the
  scanner's intensity. A big scan fills in point by point as it loads, without stopping
  the picture, and one or two stray points far out do not shrink the rest to a dot. Drop
  a scan on a layer, and the session collects and relinks it like any other file.
- **The sound as land.** The Terrain's **raised by** has a fourth choice, **the sound** : each
  moment of the spectrum is laid down at your feet as a strip of hills (bass to the left,
  treble to the right) and drifts away to the horizon at **sound speed**, the land jumping
  forward on the hits (**surge on hits**). It wears every Terrain look : terraces, contour
  lines, snow, a lake. Three presets, two Generate themes (Sounding Land, Night Sounding) and
  two master chains (Sounding Chart, Long Memory).
- **Stems : each layer hears its own sound.** With an interface or a virtual cable of more than
  two channels, an audio modulator (and an envelope's FIRE) can listen **FROM** one pair
  (`input · IN 3+4`) or one channel (`input · IN 5`) of it, whatever the audio tab itself
  listens to. Send each track to its own outputs and each layer follows its own.
  Nothing changes in the sessions you have.
- **Complex Output : map several projectors at once.** `Shift+O` (or **COMPLEX OUTPUT** in the
  Output page) opens a window with two steps. In **Screens**, give each projector its screen, at its
  own size; **identify** puts a number on every display, **split** cuts the picture over several with
  an overlap already blended. In **Map**, take any part of the picture, or one layer alone, and put it
  where you want on that screen : four corners in true perspective, a grid, a curved grid, Bezier
  patches, or a polygon whose points you pin one by one. Masks hide or show a part, with a soft edge
  as wide as you like; soft edges make two projectors' seam disappear, with the projector's gamma and
  its black level. A list along the bottom says what is wrong and takes you to it.
  The setup stays on your computer (with named presets and files), or goes with the session.
  Brightness, opacity and on / off can be played : modulators, Macro knobs, MIDI, OSC.
  A setup from an `AdvancedOutput.xml` file can be imported. The screens follow the picture with no
  copy through the processor. Measured : 57 to 60 fps with three 4K screens, the control window in front or minimized, 0.1 to 0.4 ms of the frame.
  Nothing changes if you do not use it : one projector, its keystone and the dome work as before.
  A display the controls are on is never covered without asking.
- **Walk in the dome.** The Fulldome viewer is now a room at its real size : the Satosphère
  at 210° or 180°, or a dome of your own. Sit in the **best seat**, stand at the **center**, in
  the **back row** or on the **side**, lie down, or look from **outside**, with the eye at its
  real height. Click it and walk (**WASD** or the arrows, **Shift** runs), **fly** (F), or take a
  gamepad; a floor plan puts you anywhere. It reads the **pixels per degree** where you look and
  how fast the picture **turns around you**, marks the **seam and the zenith**, can show
  (approximately) how a bright dome washes out its own blacks, and lays the dome out flat
  (**equirect**). **Expand** it over the page or give it a **window** of its own. The picture
  is the full master now, sharp at any zoom, and it gives way by itself the moment the show
  slows.
