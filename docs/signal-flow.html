Reading the map

Where every signal enters, what it changes, and where it leaves: the control plane that runs on the CPU before each frame, the picture's path on the GPU, the sound's own paths, and the loops where the picture, the sound, the body and the network come back to change themselves. Hover or tap a block to follow its connections and see the code it comes from.

The thick line is the spine, the path the picture really takes; the small labels are bookkeeping. Secondary links, and the control plane's links to the outputs, appear when you pick a block: at rest the control plane's links to the outputs beside it are drawn as one stream. All 141 links are on the map, 56 of them shown only on a pick.

One frame, in order

The order of App.tsx's frame loop. Everything that moves is computed before the picture is drawn, on the same frame. What is read back from the picture (vision, depth, Sonify's grids, the lights) arrives a frame or two later, which is what keeps the loops stable.

1 · Choose and sync
  1. Skip the frame if the GPU is behind
  2. A scene change, the Metasurface's blend (its drawn path), or a scene being morphed into; Link pins the BPM
  3. Speed, warp, dome, output transform, flash limit, slot swaps, shader reloads
  4. Sync: the composition into every stage syncFromState
  5. Assemble's live matchers syncLiveMatchers
2 · Move
  1. The audio bus ticks; Link; the 8 modulators run; the matrix overlays them
  2. Macro glides; Vary edges fired by modulators
  3. Context light path; film seeks
  4. Coupling sets each layer's A/B mix from the sound
  5. Proximity, then the scene sequencer, then the Seq page on the bar clock (Sonify steps, key, rules)
  6. Field macros, then the World amount, then tonicity, drift, flow ↔ interruption
  7. Freeze, flicker, frame-weave, relay envelopes, Black (it fades the films' sound too)
3 · Draw
  1. Each layer: source A → its rack, B → its rack, the A/B mix, the layer rack, the Loop (delay, Loop rack, ×0.97 max)
  2. The Background and its rack
  3. The stack over the Background: blend, opacity, mask, mute / solo, group
  4. Master rack, then Vibe Palette, Context, Finalizer; film hold, damage, output shape
  5. Presentation: scene fade, freeze, Black, flash limit
  6. Output transform or dome, then the keystoned operator canvas
4 · Send, then read back
  1. Projector stream, NDI, Spout / Syphon, dome preview, recording (clean, before the keystone)
  2. MARK strip (from the canvas)
  3. Vision readback ~30 Hz, then Depth AI every 85 ms (read on later frames)
  4. The lights; the output window's keystone; HIVE (the canvas)
  5. Sonify, the Audio Layer, Space (Sonify's grids, 1–2 frames late)
  6. Between frames: OSC feedback, OSCQuery, dataflou sources, Resolume, MIDI clock

Where the loops close

Palinopsia is a feedback instrument: its output comes back in through several paths. Some close inside the app, some only through the room, the network or an agent.
